You get one MP. You get every Lord. A free iPhone and iPad app, The Lords, makes that useful by letting people find members of the House of Lords by the subjects they know most about.

The Lords was designed and built by Philip Brumpton, a product designer from Plumstead in South East London, as a companion to his app [Who Is My MP?](/press/who-is-my-mp-launch/).

The upper house is not organised by geography, so a postcode is no help. What peers do have is deep specialist knowledge, in medicine, transport, law, science, farming and much else. The Lords lets users search every current member by subject, then read their record in the same plain way: what they have said in debates, the questions they have asked, the committees they sit on and the interests they have declared.

> "Nobody has their own peer, which means every peer is potentially yours. If you care about an issue, there are members of the Lords who have spent their careers on it. The app is there to help you find them."
>
> Philip Brumpton, designer of The Lords

Like its sister app, The Lords is neutral by design, with no ratings, no league tables and no commentary. It uses UK Parliament's own published data and links every item back to the source.

The Lords is free on the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id6801590675), with more at [thelords.app](https://thelords.app).
