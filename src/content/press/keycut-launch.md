Video editors who keep a cheat sheet next to the keyboard now have an alternative. KeyCut is an iPad app that works as a colour-coded control surface for Final Cut Pro: tap a key and the real shortcut is sent to the Mac over Wi-Fi.

KeyCut was designed and built by Philip Brumpton, a product designer from Plumstead in South East London, and was the first app he shipped entirely on his own.

Each key on the iPad is grouped by colour into editing, trimming, playback, marking and tools. Press and hold any key to see what it does in plain English. A small free helper app on the Mac, KeyCut Live, listens for each tap and presses the matching shortcut, so editors can keep their hands off the keyboard or learn the shortcuts as they go.

> "Final Cut has hundreds of shortcuts and most editors use a handful. I wanted something that sits next to the Mac, shows you the whole set in colour, and does the shortcut for you until your fingers remember it."
>
> Philip Brumpton, designer of KeyCut

The app is free to download, with a single one-off unlock for the full layout and no subscription. It works on iPad and iPhone, with the KeyCut Live helper on the Mac.

> "A year ago, if I wanted to build a product I'd have needed a developer. KeyCut is designed and built by one person, on Apple's own frameworks, and costs almost nothing to run."
>
> Philip Brumpton

KeyCut is free on the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id6761501461), with more at [getkeycut.com](https://getkeycut.com).
