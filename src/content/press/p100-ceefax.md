Ceefax is back, after a fashion. P100 is a free website that recreates the BBC's teletext service, with live news, sport, weather and TV listings, in seven colours on a black background.

P100 was designed and built by Philip Brumpton, a product designer from Plumstead in South East London. It takes its name from page 100, the Ceefax index. Visitors type a three-digit page number and wait a moment, exactly as they did on the living room television.

The project began as a nod to Count Binface, whose election manifesto pledged to bring Ceefax back.

> "I have fond memories of getting home from school and prodding at the new television, which had somehow acquired news, weather, quizzes and cheap flights to Florida. Fifteen years before the web reached most homes, that was a whole world rendered in seven colours on a black background."
>
> Philip Brumpton, designer of P100

Every graphic on the site is made of text. The chunky block pictures are Unicode characters, so there is not a single image on P100, and visitors can select the pictures with their cursor. The typeface is Bedstead by Ben Harris, a public domain recreation of the chip that drew teletext characters on British televisions and on the BBC Micro.

The content is live. News refreshes every five minutes, weather every fifteen and the TV guide every hour, through a small Cloudflare service that caches each feed. The holidays page on 220 searches several deal feeds for departures from UK airports, in honour of the cheap flights that were the best thing on teletext.

> "Ceefax ran for 38 years and nobody replaced it. The rest of the Binface manifesto involves capping the price of a 99 Flake and nationalising Adele, so I went for the achievable one."
>
> Philip Brumpton

P100 is free at [p100.uk](https://p100.uk). It costs nothing to run beyond the domain.
