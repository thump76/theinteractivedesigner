"Who is my MP?" is one of the most searched questions about UK politics. A free iPhone app of the same name now answers it, and then goes further, showing what that MP has actually done in Parliament.

Who Is My MP? was designed and built by Philip Brumpton, a product designer from Plumstead in South East London who was Lead Designer at Dods Parliamentary Communications fifteen years ago.

Enter a postcode and the app finds the constituency and its MP. From there it builds a profile entirely from Parliament's own published data: how the MP has voted in the Commons, what they have said in debates, the written questions they have tabled, their registered interests, the committees they sit on and their election results. Every item links back to the source on parliament.uk.

> "The easy version of an MP app is a gotcha: expenses, attendance leagues, who turned up least. I didn't want to build that. I wanted to show what MPs actually do, without editorial framing, and let people read the record in context."
>
> Philip Brumpton, designer of Who Is My MP?

The app is deliberately neutral. Nobody is ranked or scored, and the app is honest about the limits of the data. An MP with few sitting days might also be a practising doctor, and the raw number says nothing about that.

There is no sign-up and no analytics, and the postcode a user enters stays on their phone. The app is written in SwiftUI with no third-party code, talking directly to Parliament's public APIs.

Who Is My MP? launched alongside [The Lords](/press/the-lords-launch/), a companion app for finding members of the upper house by what they specialise in. Both are free on the App Store.
